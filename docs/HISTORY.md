# History atlas (antiquity–present)

Atlas Nusantara's **History** browser has **975 source-backed records**, including **107 dated curated overviews** (105 legacy polities plus modern Indonesia and Timor-Leste), and a separate collapsible **827-entry list inventory**, across Sumatra, Java, Bali, Borneo, Sulawesi, Nusa Tenggara, Maluku, Papua and the Malay Peninsula. Neither is an exhaustive, independently verified census. The inventory counts visible alphabetical source occurrences, including aliases, homonyms and possible overlaps—not distinct kingdoms (see *Inventory gap* below). It does not claim that every named dynasty formed part of modern Indonesia. The maritime history of Nusantara also involves present-day Malaysia, Brunei and other regions.

## What readers can do

- Select `History atlas` in the layer picker or visit `/?layer=history`.
- Play the timeline from **400 BCE to the current UTC year**; use the year slider or step between milestones. The earliest archaeology marker is *not* presented as a kingdom.
- Inspect the **simultaneous**, not mutually exclusive, polities in each year; select one to read the associated region, sourced editorial summary, notable rulers and dated known reigns.
- Search the whole catalogue, including source-research dossiers, or filter to the selected year. List rows show names and compact date/research badges only; descriptions, unresolved chronology and citations belong in the detail card. Nonshrinking, content-sized rows prevent text overlap at narrow widths. Selecting a dated curated result outside the current year moves the timeline into its documented display period. Selecting a research dossier clears the map focus without inventing a timeline date or boundary. Timeline controls, playback, active-year scope and map selection return to the dated record view. Dated cards also expose their linked research dossiers in a collapsed supplemental-source section. The same query filters the separate inventory, which is collapsed by default; its links open the source. On playback the list switches to active dated polities and expired entries disappear.
- Download **GeoJSON**, **JSON**, **CSV**, or **XML** locally. GeoJSON/JSON contain one polity/source record per `features` item, with explicit review status and unknown dates preserved as null, nested bilingual narratives, rulers, source URLs, dated location references and `territory_ids`. `milestones`, `territories` (untouched dated source polygons), `rendered_territories` (coastline-clipped display polygons), `research_sources`, `discovery_inventory`, `territorial_status`, `reference_geometries` and neighboring `context_land` are separate top-level fields. Modern milestones preserve exact documented dates; year-only starts remain explicitly imprecise. UI downloads capture `snapshot_year` before loading and pause playback. Active dated features carry the same clipped polygon shown by the map, or an explicitly labelled reference `Point` when no dated polygon exists; points do not assert capitals or boundaries. Inactive and undated features keep null geometry. Programmatic all-time archives without a requested year also keep null geometry. `geometry_context` preserves roles, applicable bounds and stable source-snapshot IDs; there is no timeless historical frontier. Source territory ranges are preserved, and must be intersected with catalogue bounds before use. XML preserves this hierarchy. CSV has one row per catalogue record; research-only records have null dates and location fields. Nested CSV cells contain JSON, never extra milestone rows. The `research_dossier` CSV column is always an array, including one-item and empty arrays. `research_dossier_ids` link every feature to the full source collection. Embedded datasets retain their own source/licence metadata; no blanket licence is claimed for the combined export.

## Geography: reconstructed territory and approximate centres

For 35 legacy polities and the two modern states, the map uses dated polygon reconstructions from [Cliopatria v0.2.1](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria), Bennett et al. and the Seshat Global History Databank, [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), extracted and identity-mapped by `scripts/geo/build-history-territories.mjs`. The data was not simplified. The extractor pins the input GeoJSON SHA-256 to `fc6f0d1e4cc42f45da83522b3696db6f2cc85ec0d12259a85c89b31347b8fc0a` (upstream zip Git blob `a1e7093b64990bf97cd3c31bda96de1cafed822c`). The source is an interpretation, not a surveyed or independently verified annual frontier; its date ranges extend reconstructed snapshots between irregular source dates. Catalog dates can differ from Cliopatria, so a polygon is shown only where both agree.

Both map views now read `static/data/history-territories-render.geojson`, built by `scripts/geo/clip-history-territories.mjs`. The builder reconstructs all **617 closed SVG rings**, including **7 holes**, from the exact 38 national paths in `static/data/geometry-2025/national.json`. It projects source geometry into that same Mercator plane, unions the base map's **nonzero province fills** to remove internal seams, independently intersects each dated source polygon without simplification, then inversely projects the result. SVG draws the verified derivative directly. A redundant runtime multi-path clip caused Chromium to paint only Aceh and has been removed; build-time nonzero intersections remain authoritative. Original source polygons remain untouched in `history-territories.geojson` and in the export's `territories` field. The render artifact records both input hashes, boundary edition, operations and source licences. All display polygons are clipped to this atlas land geometry so fills and borders use the same island shapes. Modern views also carry separately attributed Timor-Leste reference land from Natural Earth 5.1.1 (public domain); it is not inserted as an Indonesian province. Historical coastlines may differ. Historical markers remain schematic rather than verified flags or capitals. Indonesia instead uses an original vector rendering of the documented national flag: red over white, equal bands, width:height 3:2, per Law 24/2009. Its approximate city marker is at Jakarta, not Natural Earth's country-label placement on Sumatra. Where no dated polygon is available they do not imply a territorial fill. Markers are approximate and may represent a site, regional core, or administrative centre; their coordinates are not uniformly verified as royal seats. All polities remain available in the list. The dataset still retains confidence classes and schematic zones as research metadata, but these zones are not drawn as territory.

The render build uses a pinned, build-only `polygon-clipping@0.15.7` dependency. Mapshaper's batch and isolated Boolean operations failed production regressions, including offshore residues; its erase cross-check also returned a false positive against independently verified geometry. No source repair, buffering, grid snapping or relaxed geometric tolerance is used. All **197** legacy source records remain intact; **187** have nonempty national-land intersections. A separate untouched **11-frame** modern source extract produces **20** qualified display features, for **207** rendered features total. Snapshot IDs depend on polity, source dates and source name, not array positions.

Rebuild and verify with:

1. `npm run build:history-render`
2. `node scripts/geo/verify-history-svg.mjs` — independent original-path winding, boundary-subsegment, hole and positive-interior checks, with frozen offshore witnesses and negative controls in the tests.
3. `npm run verify:history-render` — independent GEOS reconstruction from SVG faces and nonzero winding, checking every legacy source intersection, omitted feature and retained interior, plus modern Papua/Timor transition witnesses. The independent SVG oracle covers all 207 rendered features against the national paths plus the separate Natural Earth reference input. This optional developer check uses `uv` and pinned Shapely 2.1.2; normal app builds and Node tests do not require Python.

Polygons can overlap. Current provincial boundaries and coastlines are used only as a visual land mask, not to infer historical jurisdiction. Do not compute territory sizes, population, percentage of modern Indonesia controlled, or modern legal claims from these visualizations. Approximate original city/site coordinates remain data points, and historical sites and ports may have changed locations. The three-dimensional map drapes the source reconstructions over modern relief. Modern provincial borders are hidden in this layer; present-day terrain and coastlines do not establish historical jurisdiction.

## Modern status and transitions

`history-modern.ts` preserves sourced administrative phases and exact milestones; `history-countries.ts` adds Indonesia and Timor-Leste. Indonesia's 17 August 1945 declaration is distinct from the 27 December 1949 transfer to federal Indonesia, which excluded Dutch New Guinea. UNTEA administered that territory from 1 October 1962 until the 1 May 1963 administrative transfer. The 1969 representative-council process remains disputed; UNGA Resolution 2504 did not certify its fairness.

East Timor's 1975 invasion and unrecognized 1976 annexation are not ordinary provincial accession. The 1999 ballot, Indonesian withdrawal and UN transition precede 20 May 2002 independence. Annual maps represent the state after the year's major transition; precise dates remain available in exports. Regional status is not a comprehensive military-control map.

The pinned source has no 1945 or post-2024 frame. The 1945 view is explicitly a declaration/reference extent, not effective control; 2025 onward uses attributed reference outlines. The broad 1960–75 source frame is clipped to exclude West New Guinea before 1963; the 1976–2005 frame excludes Timor-Leste from 1999 onward. The source frames themselves are untouched. Timor-Leste reference land is excluded from Indonesia outside the qualified occupation period, preventing mixed-resolution overlap. These operations are display qualifications, not treaty-boundary verification.

Rebuild source/context inputs with `build-history-territories.mjs ... modern` and `uv run --with pyshp==2.3.1 python scripts/geo/import-history-context.py SOURCE.zip OUTPUT.geojson`. Natural Earth source SHA-256 is pinned to `ce1ac7036499a0edd641fbc093cd209a98f96a49d2eca8480aaacad35138a7f6`. Original source links and component licences accompany the data.

CSV retains structured periods (including ongoing/current-year cutoff), geometry context and source/reference provenance, so its numeric end year cannot silently imply dissolution.

Both views key animation to actual geometry, not period metadata. Changed shapes crossfade over 420 ms; unchanged shapes stay stable. Expired names become inert immediately, fade out and unmount. Reduced motion skips fades. Indonesia's white band retains a continuous red/white outline so it cannot be mistaken for uncolored land.

## Historical chronology and source criticism

The core records in `src/lib/data/history.ts`, the extended gazetteer in `src/lib/data/history-more.ts`, and additions in `src/lib/data/history-researched.ts` are **hand-edited**: each record has a stable ID, approximate start/end years, tradition, an island `region` (localized in all locales), `place` and `placeId` text, short original EN/ID narratives, named rulers (some with known dates), dated schematic zones, source URLs, and optional chronographic notes. Colonial entities are explicitly marked separately and are not grouped with dynasties.

The 2019 **Kementerian Pendidikan dan Kebudayaan** volume *Kerajaan-kerajaan Nusantara dalam sejarah: Sumatera* (Agus Aris Munandar et al.) is a useful scholarly starting point for Sumatran polities: https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/23858/. Access and usage conditions of the book are **CC BY-NC 4.0**, so the book itself and its illustrations are **not bundled** in this repository.

Other independent starting points:

- General chronology: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejarah_Nusantara (orientation only; disputed dates must be checked independently).
- Tarumanagara 5th-century inscriptions and Purnawarman: https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/26567/
- Majapahit reign of Hayam Wuruk 1350–1389: https://bintangpusnas.perpusnas.go.id/konten/BK77583/seri-sejarah-kekaisaran-dan-imperium-dunia-kerajaan-majapahit
- Sriwijaya and Kedukan Bukit inscription: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasasti_Kedukan_Bukit
- 1928 Youth Pledge and nationalist movements: https://muspada.kemenbud.go.id/sejarah-sumpah-pemuda/
- Scholarly national reference series held by the Indonesian Ministry of Home Affairs: https://bskdnlib.kemendagri.go.id/index.php?id=1433&p=show_detail
- Original BIG historical map services, **reference only**: https://geoservices.big.go.id/gis/rest/services/PTRA/Atlas_250K_MasaIndonesiaKlasik/MapServer. Redistribution of BIG rendered layers or the service's geometric data is **not** inferred to be permitted.

### Early evidence and centre research

- The [ANU synthesis on Kutai](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p69411/html/ch15s02.html) dates the Mulawarman yupa palaeographically to the second half of the fourth century; other references date them to the fifth. The 350 CE start is an approximate display bound, not a known foundation. Muara Kaman is the find area, not a proven capital.
- [Batujaya excavations](https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_2015_num_101_1_6212) document Buni-phase burials roughly in the first century BCE–second century CE. The diamond marks the approximate archaeological site, **not a kingdom or capital**, and does not imply that the whole Buni culture was confined to those dates. Earlier archaeological evidence is not proof that no polities existed.
- [UNESCO's Trowulan tentative-list entry](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5466/) identifies the Trowulan area with Majapahit's former capital. Palembang is an important [Sriwijaya archaeological centre](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/traces_of_buddhism_in_sumatra_an_archaeological_perspective.pdf), but its precise early capital remains debated. Tarumanagara's capital is not securely established; its western Java marker is an approximate regional core, not a claim about a palace location.
- Salakanagara's second-century chronology derives from contested later manuscripts; it is not inserted as an established kingdom. See [historical-evidence review](https://doi.org/10.24036/diakronika/vol23-iss1/334). Other early polities and site identifications require record-specific primary or scholarly evidence.

### Limitations

Some end dates and founding years are disputed, and traditional king lists may not line up with contemporary inscriptions. A missing known reign does not prove there was no king. Long polities may have periods of interruption or succession not represented in this overview. The events are **waypoints**, not a year-by-year documentary reconstruction. Legacy royal profiles retain their 1944 catalogue cutoff; it does **not** imply that surviving institutions dissolved. The modern-state timeline continues from 1945 to the present. Ongoing presidencies have no fabricated end year.

Editorial source-specific research notes use Indonesian and English originals. The 14-locale interface strings are provided through inlang/Paraglide, and other languages receive localized historical metadata overviews (polity, region, approximate period, and ruler names). These overviews are deliberately **not** marketed as full translations of the source-specific notes; detailed translation and historical copyediting are outstanding. Japanese currently uses existing English strings outside the newly translated History controls. **Translation QA is open**, especially for low-resource languages. The same factual historical IDs, names, dates and provenance apply across languages. Colours distinguish polity IDs rather than political or religious categories; late Islamic court titles are not retroactively imposed on earlier periods.

## Changing the dataset

1. Add one record with `id`, `name`, `from`, `to`, `summary.en`, `summary.id`, `rulers`, `zones`, and at least one **record-specific** verifiable source URL.
2. Prefer datable inscriptions, archaeological publications, critical scholarly history, museum catalogue entries and official institution reports. Use encyclopedias as navigation aids. Cite which assertions each source supports; do not guess a precise historic border.
3. If a date is disputed, add a `dateNote`; preserve competing chronologies rather than silently conflating royal lineages.
4. Place documented centres only; for uncertain influence, use `contested-influence` and date-bound it. Do not infer extent from present-day provinces or automatically enlarge an empire based on a literary list of tributaries.
5. Run `npm run check:locales`, `npm run check`, `npm test`, and `npm run build`. The history tests are under `tests/geo/history.test.mjs`.
6. Publish new or corrected historical information only after provenance and translation review.

## Architecture & performance

- The dated ESM catalogue loads with the selected history layer. The wider multi-megabyte research GeoJSON loads lazily when History opens and is cached as immutable data; its original source text is not bundled into the initial app script. The inventory is collapsed and does not mount its result links until expanded. No remote history backend, cloud database, user-query tracking, animation dependency or video media is required.
- The historical overlay projects dated Cliopatria Polygon/MultiPolygon rings through the atlas Mercator projection, preserving islands and holes. Missing polygons get an approximate centre marker in 2D, not a guessed territorial fill. Map features, labels and the textual list share the selected polity.
- Playback stops when the tab is hidden. Users can scrub directly or jump to milestones. Browser reduced-motion settings are respected by existing map controls.
- The animated history combines source reconstructions and approximate centre markers. Do not market it as an exact or comprehensive territorial animation.

## Inventory gap (research)

`static/data/history-inventory.geojson` contains **827 visible alphabetical list occurrences**, extracted by `node scripts/geo/build-history-inventory.mjs` from Indonesian Wikipedia [revision 29769449](https://id.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daftar_kerajaan_yang_pernah_ada_di_Nusantara&oldid=29769449), dated 2026-09-16. Names, leading aliases and source labels are retained; descriptions are not copied. Attribution and CC BY-SA 4.0 metadata travel with the inventory. Commented-out bullets and non-alphabetical sections are excluded. Source-occurrence IDs preserve two repeated primary labels, Padang and Tanjung: equal labels do not prove equal polities. The source category [Kerajaan di Nusantara](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kategori:Kerajaan_di_Nusantara) is another discovery route, not an independently verified census.

The earlier ignored research CSV contains 831 names without capture metadata; it is not the published source of truth. A raw count including invisible comments is misleading. The user-mentioned **782** total remains unverified. Inventory entries have `geometry`, period and summary set to `null`, no inferred rulers, and explicit `unreviewed_inventory` status. They can overlap curated records; do not add 827 and 105 to claim a distinct-kingdom total. Each candidate needs record-specific evidence, dates, alias resolution and editorial review before promotion to the dated catalogue.

The complete source pass processes every one of the **827** visible list occurrences, checks the root Wikipedia category's direct members, and indexes all **365 body pages** of Hägerdal's licensed genealogy PDF. It yields **939 research dossiers**: the 827 list entries, 41 additional category-article records and 71 additional genealogy-heading records. Exact-name navigation links associate 71 dossiers with dated records; they do not assert historical continuity or globally resolve aliases. With the two modern state profiles, the combined browser/export has **975 records**, not 975 independently verified distinct kingdoms.

Among the 827 list entries, **230** have located article sources, **221** have exact-normalized genealogy-heading/cross-reference matches, and **389** have name mentions in the five supplied PDFs. These counts overlap. **372** had no additional corpus match before the web pass; another **124** had only name mentions in books, not an identified article or genealogy. PDF name mentions are not identity or factual corroboration; common-name ambiguity remains explicit. Category membership, encyclopedia infobox fields and traditional genealogy statements are also not independent verification. All **496** source-gap records received targeted native-provider web research after Bing through the supplied Camofox endpoint repeatedly returned unrelated results and Google returned an unusual-traffic HTTP 429. No CAPTCHA or restriction was bypassed; the user approved alternative sources. The notes contain **313** brief original factual summaries and **183** uncertainty-only findings. These are search leads, not full-text verification or confirmation that every name denotes a kingdom; places, customary jurisdictions, vassal domains and ambiguous terms remain distinguished. The failed Membora query was retried successfully, while its spelling variants and kingdom status remain unresolved. Seven identity ambiguities are explicit, including the repeated Padang/Tanjung labels, Kadiri/Kediri versus Lombok, the two Panei entries, and Samprangan versus its modern locality.

Research dossiers expose attributed Indonesian article extracts, reported fields, page-cited CC BY 4.0 genealogy text, book-page mention references, and the additional search notes and citations. Copyrighted local-book text and absolute filesystem paths are not published. Source-language extracts are clearly distinguished from translated interface copy.

Reproduce this pass with `uv run --with pypdf python scripts/geo/index-history-books.py [PDF-directory]`, then `uv run --with pypdf python scripts/geo/research-history.py`. Network responses and local source text are cached only under ignored `research/history/`. Sanitized page references and original web research notes are preserved in `content/history/book-mentions.json` and `content/history/web-research.json`, so a fresh clone does not lose enrichment when those private caches are absent. The genealogy download is SHA-1 checked against the pinned Dataverse V3 file before extraction. Machine-readable raw source claims remain separate from dated/ruler facts; charts are not automatically flattened into invented reigns.

The seven new curated overviews are Adonara, Amanuban, Amanatun, Amfoan, Insana, Amarasi and Maubara. Their intervals describe documentary/display bounds, not proven foundations or dissolution. Modern locality proxies, including weak Mapcarta references, are explicitly qualified rather than presented as dated capitals. Several research publications were accessible through indexed excerpts only; specialist and direct-source review remains open. Sulu was researched but not added because its proposed centre lies outside the current atlas coordinate scope. A unified Alor kingdom was not invented from evidence for multiple coastal domains.

[GLOBALISE Kerajaan2 Indonesia](https://doi.org/10.34894/CBO80N) is a CC BY 4.0 PDF genealogy inventory, not a machine-readable 782-row kingdom census. The separate [GLOBALISE polities workbook](https://doi.org/10.34894/SIAUPO), CC BY-SA 4.0, covers VOC-related early-modern entities across regions; its rows and duplicates were not verified here, and it was not imported.

## Source quality (known limit)

The bundled Cliopatria extracts match 37 of the 107 dated records, including the two modern states; the other 70 have no matching dated polygon. Modern source gaps and oversized period spans require the qualifications described above. Sukadana's dated features are mapped to the catalogue's Tanjungpura/Sukadana record only for the source's 1619–1833 span; the continuity between early Tanjungpura and later Sukadana is debated. Even for a matched polity, some years have no matching source feature. Clipping a polygon to modern island shapes removes offshore spill but cannot repair an inland border, recover an omitted island, or prove political control. Matching a modern province or an entire island to a historic kingdom would create unsupported boundaries.

Research leads for expanding coverage include [BIG's classical atlas service](https://geoservices.big.go.id/gis/rest/services/PTRA/Atlas_250K_MasaIndonesiaKlasik/MapServer), whose Majapahit layers are reference-only until redistribution rights are established, and [GLOBALISE's Kerajaan2 Indonesia](https://doi.org/10.34894/CBO80N), an inventory rather than a polygon dataset. [Cliopatria's methods paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9) describes its reconstruction and uncertainty. Maritime polities can have networks of ports and tributaries rather than a continuous territorial frontier; see [Mapping the Acehnese Past](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/25958), [Ternate's colonial-era connections](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/malay-magic-on-the-colonial-frontier-ternaten-spellcasters-and-hostages-of-war-in-seventeenthcentury-manila/1506DC94C68193A55AEBE70F240E1EEE), and [Papuan relationships with Tidore](https://asianethnology.org/article/155043-papua-matters-remembering-origins-ethno-historical-reconstructions-and-regional-politics-in-tidore-indonesia) for regional examples. A November 2026 OpenHistoricalMap Overpass search for 37 names from the uncovered catalogue did not find kingdom relations by their primary names. A subsequent English/Indonesian name search across those candidates found only Tidore relations 2948780–2948785, whose primary name is in Jawi. Their geometry cites a [CC BY-SA map of Tidore in 1800](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sultanate_of_Tidore_in_1800.png), while some relations extend the geometry into much earlier centuries. These relations have not been incorporated: derivative licensing and period-specific historical evidence require review. A name-based query alone does not establish that a territory is absent. More coverage requires period-specific, redistributable map evidence and historical review for each identity and date range; do not treat place names alone as proof of a polygon.

Every dated curated record cites sources; many include a polity-specific article or institutional page, while some use broader regional reference volumes. Research-only dossiers can have list-level or provisional search citations and are not claimed to have equivalent verification. Many extended-gazetteer records cite **only encyclopedia articles** that were opened during compilation; only some add Kemendikdasmen, Balai Arkeologi or museum sources. Dates that a source gives only as a century or tradition use the earliest/latest sourced year and carry a bilingual `dateNote`. A specialist should review the extended records before the layer is presented as authoritative.

## WorldStatesmen.org (rulers, capitals, flags)

[WorldStatesmen.org](https://www.worldstatesmen.org/Indonesia.htm) (© Ben Cahoon) and its subpages [Traditional States part 1](https://www.worldstatesmen.org/Indonesia_princely_states1.html) and [part 2](https://www.worldstatesmen.org/Indonesia_princely_states2.html) are used as a **reference source** for:

- reign start and end years of rulers of traditional states and of the Dutch East India Company governors-general, with reign years only (names and years are facts; the site's prose is not copied);
- capitals and the existence dates of Dutch-era traditional states;
- candidate historical flag images, which are **not displayed** because permission has not been confirmed.

**Use and attribution:** WorldStatesmen's homepage asks prospective non-commercial users to notify its editor of the intended use, and requires express written permission for commercial use. Its introduction also asks readers to contact the owner about use or linking. Historical facts can be cited as research leads; attribution alone does not grant rights to copy text or images. Confirm applicable rights and any requested permission before release. Most flags are credited by WorldStatesmen to third parties, so each image needs its own rights check.

**Flags:** No WorldStatesmen images are displayed or copied. Indonesia's separately sourced national flag is rendered locally as simple vector bands. Obtain permission or find independently licensed, historically verified images before adding flags.
